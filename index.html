L Stop

L Stop is a free daily game by James Robinson.

Guess today's Chicago L station in six tries. A new one every day. Every guess gets three clues back. Which train lines both stations are on. About how many stops apart they are. Which direction the answer is from your guess.

      Each guess shows up on the map as a dot with its number on it, 1 for your first guess. Today's station is marked too once the game ends.

      Want more than one a day? The practice file gives you as many stations as you like, works offline, and costs $3.

      How it works

      The L is Chicago's train system, eight lines named by color. Type a station name and press the Guess button. Six tries a day. Each row names the train lines that stop at both your guess and today's station. It also tells you about how many stops apart the two stations are, as a range like 4 to 6, by the shortest ride even if that means changing trains. You also get which way today's station is from your guess, north, south-east and so on. A new station starts at midnight Chicago time. When you're done you can play any earlier day from the list under your result. Only today's game counts toward how many days in a row you've won.

      When the game ends you can press Share. That copies your result, one square for each guess, so you can paste it into a text or a post. Yellow means that guess shared a line with today's station, white means it didn't.

      Station names and lines come from Wikipedia's list of Chicago L stations. The sentence about each day's station is written from facts on its Wikipedia page. Not connected with the Chicago Transit Authority, which runs the L.
